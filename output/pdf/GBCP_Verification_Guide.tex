\section*{1. Claim and conventions}
The original problem asks which $\delta>0$ admit nonzero real polynomials
$x,y,p$, all strictly Hurwitz stable, such that
\[
p(s)=(s^2-2\delta s+1)x(s)+(s^2-1)y(s),\qquad \deg y\le\deg x.
\]
Strictly stable means every complex root has negative real part;
nonzero constants are allowed, and no degree or leading-sign normalization
is imposed. Put $q=(1-\delta)/(1+\delta)$ and
$\delta(q)=(1-q)/(1+q)$. Write $P(q)$ for original polynomial feasibility.
For $0<q<1$, $A(q)$ means that a real-symmetric holomorphic function on the
unit disc has exactly the simple zero $0$ and exactly the simple $1$-points
$\pm i\sqrt q$.

The concrete real $Q=\texttt{analyticQ}$ is the infimum of the analytic
feasible parameters. The main theorem proves $0<Q<1/2$ and
\[
 A(q)\iff Q\le q,\qquad P(q)\iff Q<q\quad(0<q<1).
\]
Consequently $\mathcal A=(0,\Delta)$, where $\Delta=(1-Q)/(1+Q)$, and
$\Delta\notin\mathcal A$. For every natural precision $m$, a certified
finite search yields rational $L,R$ with
\[
L<Q<R,\qquad R-L<2^{-m}.
\]
No test deciding whether an arbitrary input equals $Q$ is required.

\section*{2. Actual dependency structure}
The arrows below indicate mathematical dependencies, not a chronological
research narrative.
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{c}
Original polynomial witness $\longrightarrow$ analytic witness\\
$\downarrow$\\
Four zero-free analytic factors and explicit uniform coefficient boxes\\
$\downarrow$\\
$A(q)\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \forall N\,H_N(q)$, on $0<q\le9/16$\\
$\downarrow$\\
Compactness/threshold attainment + finite positive/negative certificates\\
$\downarrow$\\
Concrete two-test-point search and certified enclosures of $Q$
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Two additional branches are essential. Strict analytic slack, Module 3
approximation, and finite Cayley homogenization give a true
\texttt{OriginalWitness}. Independently, original-polynomial strict
improvement plus the forward map excludes the polynomial endpoint.
The parameter bound $\delta<1$ is proved directly from original witnesses.
These branches combine with the analytic threshold to give the original
feasible-set theorem. The algorithm does \emph{not} itself imply endpoint
exclusion.

\section*{3. What the release does not claim}
No closed form, algebraicity, minimal polynomial, special-function expression,
practical complexity guarantee, fixed-$N$ threshold criterion, or generic
black-box equality detector is asserted. Independent expert validation is
requested; kernel verification does not establish novelty or community acceptance.

\section*{4. Ten high-value checkpoints}
All names below are actual declarations. Unless a longer prefix is shown,
their namespace is \texttt{BelgianChocolate}; paths are relative to the repository.

\itemhead{1}{Original polynomial to analytic}
For $0<q<1$, $P(q)\Rightarrow A(q)$, including every actual-degree case.\\
\decl{polynomialFeasible\_implies\_analyticFeasible}\\
\path{BCPThreshold/PolynomialToAnalytic.lean}. Role: the necessary direction.

\itemhead{2}{Uniform all-functions coefficient bounds}
For $0<q\le(3/4)^2$, every factor coefficient has norm at most the explicit
rational $C_j$, independently of the function and truncation order.\\
\decl{Schottky.coefficientBound}\\
\path{BCPThreshold/CoefficientBounds.lean}. Role: a common compact box.

\itemhead{3}{Both directions of all-orders realization}
For $0<q\le9/16$, $A(q)\iff\forall N\,H_N(q)$; the reverse direction
constructs compatible infinite coefficients, convergent series, and exact identities.\\
\decl{analyticFeasible\_iff\_real\_all\_levels}\\
\path{BCPThreshold/FinalMain.lean}; implementation:
\decl{Route1.RealAllOrders.allOrders\_realization} and
\decl{Route1.RealAllOrders.analyticFeasible\_all\_levels}, in
\path{BCPThreshold/RealAllOrdersRealization.lean}.
For rational input the matching declaration is
\decl{Route1.AllOrders.analyticFeasible\_iff\_allOrders}.

\itemhead{4}{Positive certificate existence}
If $0<r<q<1$, $q$ is rational, and $A(r)$, a genuine finite rational
polynomial certificate exists; exact identity and strict margin are preserved.\\
\decl{strictAnalyticFeasible\_positiveCertificate}\\
\path{BCPThreshold/RationalPositiveCertificate.lean}.

\itemhead{5}{Negative finite-search completeness}
If some rational-parameter finite level is infeasible, some finite stage
of the actual Boolean search succeeds. Soundness is separately proved.\\
\decl{Route1.TrueHierarchy.hierarchyNegative\_complete}\\
\path{BCPThreshold/RCFRoute1/TrueHierarchy.lean}.

\itemhead{6}{Algorithm termination and strict precision}
Distinct trisection points ensure finite success without deciding equality
to $Q$; iterations preserve strict brackets and contract by $2/3$.\\
\decl{ThresholdData.precisionInterval\_correct}\\
\path{BCPThreshold/CertifiedAlgorithm.lean}; concrete realization:
\decl{analyticThresholdData} in \path{BCPThreshold/ConcreteAnalyticCertificates.lean}.

\itemhead{7}{Strict-slack original realization}
$0<r<q<1$ and $A(r)$ imply $P(q)$, via real polynomial Cayley
homogenization with exact identity and actual degree.\\
\decl{strictSlackRealization}\\
\path{BCPThreshold/StrictSlackRealization.lean}.

\itemhead{8}{Original strict improvement}
For $0<q<1$, a polynomial witness yields one at some $0<q'<q$.
Affine substitution/coefficient mixing handles leading cancellation.\\
\decl{polynomialFeasible\_strict\_improvement}\\
\path{BCPThreshold/PolynomialStrictImprovement.lean}.

\itemhead{9}{Endpoint separation}
$A(Q)$ but $\neg P(Q)$; exclusion depends on checkpoint 8, not on
an infimum definition alone.\\
\decl{endpoint\_separation}\\
\path{BCPThreshold/EndpointFormalization.lean}.

\itemhead{10}{Concrete original-problem main theorem}
Combines all parameter ranges, the original feasible interval, the endpoint,
the real hierarchy, and arbitrary-precision rational enclosures.\\
\decl{belgianChocolate\_exact\_algorithmic\_solution}\\
\path{BCPThreshold/FinalMain.lean}. No pending interface is a premise.

\section*{5. Reproduce the release}
Install Git, \texttt{elan}, Python 3 and Bash (Git Bash on Windows).
Lean 4.33.0 is selected by \texttt{lean-toolchain}; Mathlib 4.33.0 is pinned
to commit \texttt{db584cd6d46c92f209a44c0f1c829460d327499d}.
\begin{quote}\footnotesize\ttfamily
git clone \textbackslash\\
\url{https://github.com/chengxuandi/belgian-chocolate-problem-lean-proof-20260910210030-init.git}\\
cd belgian-chocolate-problem-lean-proof-20260910210030-init\\
git checkout v1.0.0-proof-candidate\\
lake exe cache get\\
./verify\_bcp.sh\\
lake build BCPThreshold
\end{quote}
The cache command obtains compiled dependencies; it does not replace project
proof checking. The script builds all BCP modules serially in dependency order,
including standalone audits, then the root and \texttt{FinalAudit.lean}.
It exits on failure. The last explicit build is an optional recheck.
Expected final output is \texttt{BCP FORMAL VERIFICATION: PASS}.

The checked local source has 57 Lean files including the root, and the audit
examines 1,551 imported project declarations. The source scan reports
\texttt{ZERO\_SORRY\_CHECK=PASS} and
\texttt{PROJECT\_AXIOM\_DECLARATIONS=NONE}. For the main theorem,
\texttt{\#print axioms} reports only
\[
\texttt{[propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]}.
\]
\texttt{FinalAudit.lean} checks the required names are real theorem declarations
and rejects unexpected transitive axioms, including dependencies hidden through
private declarations. Logs are written under \path{.lake/bcp-verification/}.
Nonfatal linter/deprecation warnings may remain.

\section*{6. Review routes}
\textbf{Control theory.} Begin with \path{BCPThreshold/Definitions.lean}.
Check the original quantifiers, strict stability, parameter inverses,
forward Cayley map, strict-slack reverse construction, and actual-degree
handling in endpoint improvement. In particular do not replace the original
problem by a controller class with extra structural assumptions.

\textbf{Complex analysis.} Inspect the literal \texttt{DiscFunction} definition,
the four-factor equivalence, uniformity of coefficient bounds, and the
compact realization. Pay special attention to compatible coefficients,
convergence on the full open disc, inverse identities, and simple prescribed
fibres. Lean proves the coefficient estimate by the coarse Schottky/cosine-lift
route; the paper gives a classical Hempel-density argument.

\textbf{Lean/formal methods.} Start at \path{BCPThreshold/FinalMain.lean} and
\path{BCPThreshold/FinalAudit.lean}. Follow the concrete interface bundle,
check there is no realization assumption, and reproduce the audit from the
tagged source. The original \texttt{Admissible} definition is the primary
modeling checkpoint, not just the final theorem's name.

\section*{7. Important coverage boundary}
The main theorem and its actual dependency graph are checked; the release
does not claim a line-for-line formalization of every paper assertion.
General CAD and the displayed Hempel density theorem are not assumed as
project axioms: Lean uses separately proved alternative mechanisms.
The paper's algebraic-minimum/non-stabilization discussion is not a separately
exported Lean theorem. Finite searches are executable and their success is proved;
the total mathematical iterator uses \texttt{Nat.find} and remains
\texttt{noncomputable}. No standalone native threshold calculator is supplied.
