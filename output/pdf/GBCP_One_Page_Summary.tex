{\LARGE\color{ink}Belgian Chocolate Problem}\\[4pt]
{\Large A Proposed Exact Algorithmic Solution}\\[6pt]
Xuandi Cheng \hfill Proof candidate v1.0.0 --- 13 September 2026

\textbf{The problem.}
Determine all $\delta>0$ for which nonzero real polynomials $x,y,p$ exist with
every root strictly in the left half-plane, $\deg y\le\deg x$, and
\[
p(s)=(s^2-2\delta s+1)x(s)+(s^2-1)y(s).
\]
No bound on degree or special factorization is imposed. Nonzero constants
are permitted. This is the polynomial simultaneous-stabilization formulation.

\textbf{Main result.}
Set $q=(1-\delta)/(1+\delta)$. The candidate identifies a real
$Q\in(0,1/2)$ through a certified, terminating arbitrary-precision algorithm:
for each natural $m$, it returns rational $L,R$ with
\[
L<Q<R,\qquad R-L<2^{-m}.
\]
Writing $\Delta=(1-Q)/(1+Q)$, the original feasible set is exactly
$\mathcal A=(0,\Delta)$, and $\Delta$ is infeasible.
For $0<q<1$, analytic feasibility is $Q\le q$, whereas original polynomial
feasibility is $Q<q$. The analytic endpoint is attained; the polynomial
endpoint is independently excluded.

\textbf{Core mechanism.}
The analytic class consists of real-symmetric holomorphic functions on the
unit disc with exactly the simple zero $0$ and the two simple $1$-points
$\pm i\sqrt q$. Explicit uniform four-factor coefficient bounds yield
\[
\text{analytic feasibility}\ \Longleftrightarrow\
\text{every finite semialgebraic level }H_N(q)\text{ is feasible}
\quad(0<q\le9/16).
\]
Compactness supplies compatible infinite coefficients and exact analytic
identities. Finite negative obstructions and finite rational positive
certificates give searches on both strict sides. Two distinct trisection
points prevent endpoint equality from blocking termination.
Strict-slack polynomial realization and an actual-degree-preserving
perturbation complete the original interface.
